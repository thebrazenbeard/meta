# Self-developing metacognition: adversarial study contract

**Question:** Can a system improve its own *method-selection or method-construction policy* from feedback, with genuine later-use benefits, rather than repeat a developer-supplied answer or a reflection narrative?

Precommit the frozen executable methods, source hashes, prediction time, method-call witness, cases, selection rule, evaluation rules and budgets. Keep held-out labels in a separate curator-controlled process/permission domain; never mount labels, evaluator sources, credentials or unrestricted network in the candidate. Use seed-controlled randomized, no-revision, user-guided, reflection-only, and abstention baselines. Score accuracy, cost, calibration, coverage, negative transfer, shift robustness and cold restart retention. Check for later-use after the revision was frozen.

**Strongest counterexamples:** label-key closure can yield perfect scores without learning; an admitted learned-influence receipt can occur with zero method calls; two methods can share the same human-readable label; a development-set win can fail on shifted tasks. Explicitly test these rather than hiding them in aggregate scores.

The included toy selector is intentionally limited: developer-provided methods, same-process outcomes, no independently blind evaluation. It is infrastructure for writing tests, not evidence of self-generated cognition. Do not claim method discoverability, independent proof, canonical memory writes, or authority to change a production cognitive policy.

A qualified experiment would require a newly frozen improvement proposal with a code/config digest, observed matching method invocation in an independent candidate process, fair external evaluation, improvement on independently held held-outs, and no material regression under negative-transfer probes.

## Distribution-shift counterexample (new control)

Record *separate* near-domain and shifted-domain performance, compared to the identical predeclared baseline method. The toy `domain_shift_probe` intentionally shows a 100% to 0% collapse when parity labels reverse. Do not pool both domains into a single average and announce "learning". An adaptive recovery claim would require a genuinely changed selector with committed identity, independent feedback and favorable later unseen evaluations, not a detector printing `negative_transfer=True`.
